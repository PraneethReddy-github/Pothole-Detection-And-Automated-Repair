The present disclosure relates to a pothole detection and repair system (102) and method (200). The system (102) includes an
image acquisition unit (104) configured to acquire visual data of a road surface, including at least one of live video, recorded
footage, or static images. A processor (106) processes acquired visual data to detect one or more potholes, determine dimensions
and volume of each detected pothole, and associate each pothole with geolocation data provided by a global positioning receiver
(112). Based on determined dimensions, the processor (106) generates control signals to actuate a driving unit (136) of a
dispensing assembly (110), thereby dispensing a calculated volume of filler material into each detected pothole. The processor
(106) further evaluates repair cost based on filler material utilised, transmits repair details with before-and-after visual data to a
computing device (142), and generates resurfacing recommendations when a threshold number of potholes are identified.

BACKGROUND
[0002]

Background description includes information that may be useful in

understanding the present invention. It is not an admission that any of the
information provided herein is prior art or relevant to the presently claimed
10

invention, or that any publication specifically or implicitly referenced is prior art.
[0003]

Road infrastructure is an essential component of transportation

systems, supporting the movement of goods, services, and people across regions;
however, road surfaces are exposed to environmental conditions, varying vehicular
loads, and weather effects, which gradually lead to the formation of surface
15

irregularities such as potholes that affect traffic flow and increase maintenance
requirements. Technologies for road maintenance include visual inspection
methods, sensor-based monitoring, and manual repair operations, yet these
approaches often involve delays, limited integration, and reduced precision in
identifying the location and extent of surface defects, which can result in extended

20

maintenance cycles and increased operational effort.
[0004]

Existing techniques for detecting road defects include periodic

surveying, manual inspection, or post-reporting of damaged areas. These
techniques provide limited information regarding the size and volume of potholes
and rely on human intervention for assessment and repair planning; while some
25

automated or image-based systems have been explored, their use in large-scale road
maintenance is often constrained by processing requirements, equipment
dependence, or lack of integration with repair and reporting systems, making realtime intervention and coordinated planning challenging.
[0005]

30

Accurate estimation of repair material requirements, tracking of

repaired areas, and systematic reporting are additional aspects that are not fully

2

addressed in current practices, leading to inefficiencies in planning and resource
management.
[0006]

Therefore, there is a need for a solution that can improve

identification and assessment of potholes, facilitate timely repair operations,
5

provide structured reporting of repair activities, and optimize use of maintenance
resources while maintaining operational efficiency.

In an embodiment, a visual data analysis module (120) may be

configured to receive and process the acquired visual data using a pothole detection
30

model to detect one or more potholes on the road surface. The visual data analysis
module (120) is further configured to identify irregularities in the texture, shading,

10

or contour of the road surface that are indicative of a pothole and to output the
corresponding boundaries of each detected pothole. By analysing the detected
boundaries, the visual data analysis module (120) determines dimensions and
volume of each pothole, including evaluation of width, length, and depth
5

parameters.
[0035]

The pothole detection model implemented within the visual data

analysis module may employ one or more machine learning techniques, computer
vision algorithms, or image segmentation methods to enhance accuracy and
reliability in varying lighting and weather conditions. The visual data analysis
10

module (120) may also normalize the acquired visual data to minimise the effects
of noise, shadows, or camera vibrations during acquisition.
[0036]

In an exemplary embodiment, when the image acquisition unit (104)

captures live video of a road segment, the visual data analysis module (120) may
detect a dark irregular patch with distinct edges that differ from the surrounding
15

road texture. The visual data analysis module (120) identifies patch as a pothole,
traces its perimeter to determine the boundary, and subsequently estimates its
dimensions as approximately 40 centimeters in width, 60 centimeters in length, and
12 centimeters in depth. Using these values, the visual data analysis module (120)
calculates approximate volume of the pothole, which may further be used by the

20

dispensing control module to release a proportional amount of filler material for
effective repair.
[0037]

In an embodiment, a geolocation mapping module (122) may be

configured to associate each detected pothole with geolocation data, the geolocation
data being provided by a global positioning receiver (112). The geolocation
25

mapping module (122) ensures that every pothole identified by the visual data
analysis module (120) is tagged with precise geographic coordinates, thereby
enabling accurate localization and record keeping of road surface defects.
[0038]

In addition, geolocation mapping module (122) may further refine

received raw coordinates by applying error correction techniques, such as
30

differential GPS adjustments or averaging over multiple readings, to improve
positional accuracy. This association of pothole detection with location data enables

11

systematic cataloging of potholes and supports repair planning on both small and
large scales. For instance, upon detection of a pothole measuring 40 centimeters in
width, 60 centimeters in length, and 12 centimeters in depth, the geolocation
mapping module may tag the pothole with coordinates corresponding to latitude
5

28.6139° N and longitude 77.2090° E. This geotag is further stored along with
dimensional and repair information in the memory (108), and may also be
transmitted to the computing device (142) for centralized monitoring. By linking
detection with geolocation, the system (102) ensures that municipal authorities or
road maintenance agencies can track the exact position of each pothole, schedule

10

timely interventions, and prevent duplication of repair efforts. This integration of
pothole detection and geolocation facilitates targeted repairs and enhances
transparency, accountability, and efficiency in road maintenance operations.
[0039]

In an embodiment, repair control module (124) may be configured

to generate control signals for repair execution and reporting. The repair control
15

module (124) interprets dimensional data of each pothole, including width, length,
and depth, as determined by the visual data analysis module, and translates this
assessment into actionable instructions for the dispensing assembly. These control
signals are communicated to the driving unit (136) of the dispensing assembly
(110), which in turn adjusts the dispensing flow rate and regulates the release of

20

filler material into the pothole. This repair control module (124) ensures that the
calculated volume of filler material is precisely matched to the actual dimensions
and volume of each detected pothole. By dynamically adjusting dispensing flow
rates and volumes, the repair control module (124) minimises material wastage
while maintaining uniformity in repair quality across varying pothole sizes.

25

Additionally, the repair control module (124) can generate structured reporting data
related to the repair process, such as filler volume used, dispensing duration, and
execution status, which may be transmitted to the computing device (142) for
monitoring and recordkeeping.
[0040]

30

In an exemplary embodiment, when it is determined that a pothole

is relatively deep and wide, the repair control module (124) generates control
signals that cause the driving unit (136) to increase the dispensing flow rate and

12

volume, ensuring that the pothole is filled to an adequate level. Conversely, if the
pothole is identified as shallow and narrow, the repair control module (124)
proportionally reduces the flow rate, dispensing only the required amount of
material. Such automated regulation enables precise filling with minimal wastage
5

of filler material, thereby ensuring both operational efficiency and consistent repair
quality.
[0041]

10

monitoring, and auditing. This enables transparent tracking of resource utilisation

15

and provides maintenance authorities with verifiable records of repair activities.
[0042]

In an exemplary embodiment, when a deep pothole requires a larger

quantity of filler material, the module calculates a higher repair cost proportional to
the volume used. Conversely, for a smaller shallow pothole, the module records a
lower repair cost. The reporting function may include attaching pre-repair and post20

repair images along with exact geolocation coordinates, allowing municipal
authorities to remotely verify repair completion, assess maintenance efficiency, and
plan budgeting for future road repair operations.
[0043]

5

The present disclosure relates to a pothole detection and repair system (102) and
method (200). The system (102) includes an image acquisition unit (104)
configured to acquire visual data of a road surface, including at least one of live
video, recorded footage, or static images. A processor (106) processes acquired
visual data to detect one or more potholes, determine dimensions and volume of

10

each detected pothole, and associate each pothole with geolocation data provided
by a global positioning receiver (112). Based on determined dimensions, the
processor (106) generates control signals to actuate a driving unit (136) of a
dispensing assembly (110), thereby dispensing a calculated volume of filler
material into each detected pothole. The processor (106) further evaluates repair

15

cost based on filler material utilised, transmits repair details with before-and-after
visual data to a computing device (142), and generates resurfacing
recommendations when a threshold number of potholes are identified.
