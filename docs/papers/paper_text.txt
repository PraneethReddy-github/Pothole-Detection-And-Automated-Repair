I

ABSTRACT
Potholes are hazardous to the users of the road, lead to damage on vehicles,
and are expensive to repair. The project suggests a pothole detection and
instantaneous repair system automated with a Raspberry Pi 3 and a Pi Camera, to
improve road maintenance efficiency while avoiding unnecessary material use. When
taking a picture of a pothole, the photo and its metadata (e.g., GPS location,
timestamp, and size) are recorded on a Google Cloud server for documentation and
analysis. Pothole detection is done in real time with deep learning-based object
detection methods. Local computer vision methods are used to estimate depth and
volume, enabling the system to compute the quantity of fill material required. A
servo-motor-powered filling mechanism, mounted at the front, dispenses the needed
material automatically into the potholes detected. Automating both detection and
filling, the system minimizes material utilization, manual efforts, and helps achieve
effective, data-based road repairs at negligible expense.

INTRODUCTION

growth of the economy and development of society. However, when the traffic cycle
volumes rise and the environmental conditions change, roads are always under threat
of being worn down by usage throughout day and night. Along with the road
degradation, in this project, we have potholes, and frequent and potentially difficult
problems arise most often within maintenance. These road deformities not only pose
physical challenges or discomforts to the people who use the roads, but they represent
the physical manifestations of road infrastructure deficits of the road network of the
country. Further, they pose serious risks to other roadway users, although they are
also caused by changing car maintenance expenses and fuel economy, that relates to
more than a single front of the economy. Control techniques used in applying the

LITERATURE REVIEW
The aim is to improve accessibility [1] for the visually impaired by
implementing a real-time pothole detection system. Through the integration of
YOLOv5 into a mobile application, the system offers auditory and tactile feedback,
assisting users in safely traversing uneven road surfaces. The model attains an image
accuracy of 82.7% and a real- time detection rate of 30 FPS, providing fast and
accurate pothole detection. The solution's portable and lightweight make it a
convenient AI-based assistive tool for enhancing mobility and independence.
A state-of-the-art [2] pothole detection model based on YOLOv8, which
achieves 95.82% mAP, 0.95 precision and 0.97 recall is presented. The system
performs well in both daytime and nighttime conditions, offering strong real-time
detection with low latency. A Graphical User Interface (GUI) is added to facilitate
user interaction through real-time monitoring and automatic reporting to maintenance
teams. This method greatly enhances road safety by reducing accident risks, and it is
an extremely efficient and scalable pothole detection solution.
Development of an AIoT-based pothole detection [3] system that utilizes
computer vision, AI-on-edge technology, and real-time map visualization has been
done in this paper. With the help of YOLOv5m, the model records 91.8% mean
Average Precision (mAP) and 82.95% accuracy, providing effective pothole detection.
The system facilitates geotagging of potholes automatically, enabling transport
authorities to plan repairs with priority and maximize maintenance operations.
Through the use of edge computing, the solution operates independently of
cloud-based processing, which renders it more scalable and economical for
deployment in real-world applications.

heavily on large, annotated datasets for training. Hybrid approaches, which integrate
2D, 3D, and machine learning techniques, have demonstrated the highest accuracy
and robustness by leveraging multi-modal data. Despite these advancements,

A comprehensive comparison of CNN-based models for pothole detection
under real-world adverse conditions has been conducted, focusing on the performance
of region-based CNN (R-CNN) and You Only Look Once (YOLO) architectures. [8]

5.5 Depth Estimation for Volume Calculation
In addition to simple surface detection, the system has an advanced depth
estimation feature to determine the actual severity of every pothole. The width and
height are first obtained from the bounding box dimensions given by the YOLOv12
nano model upon object detection. To estimate depth, the system makes use of the
MIDAS (Monocular Depth Estimation) algorithm, which is a deep learning method
advanced enough to be able to estimate depth from one image without stereo vision or
LIDAR input. This algorithm allows for estimation of depth even on low-powered
hardware such as Raspberry Pi while still keeping the system in real-time processing
mode. Through the union of width, height, and depth, the system determines the
approximate volume of every pothole via the straightforward yet efficient formula:
Volume = Width × Height × Depth. This volumetric determination enables road
maintenance crews to plan repairs not just according to the occurrence of potholes but
also in relation to their relative size and potential hazard, resulting in better resource
allocation. In addition, volume calculations aid in the computation of the amount of

CONCLUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE
The creation of this pothole detection and reporting system represents a key
milestone in the quest for intelligent road upkeep and intelligent city infrastructure.
Centered on the performance of the system is the application of the YOLOv12 Nano
model, a performance-optimized variant of the YOLO (You Only Look Once) object
detection model, explicitly designed to run in low resource environments. Through
the use of YOLOv12 Nano, the system strikes a very efficient balance between
detection precision, processing speed, and model efficiency, which is essential to
maintaining real-time responsiveness on small, low-power edge devices such as the
Raspberry Pi 3. In contrast to its larger equivalents or previous YOLO variants that
tend to demand powerful GPUs and large memory resources, YOLOv12 Nano is
optimized for speed and accuracy without sacrificing detection quality. This enables
seamless, real-time field operation, and hence it is best suited for mobile or embedded
applications. The system thus provides portability as well as cost-effectiveness in
addition to providing tremendous potential for large-scale deployment in various
urban and rural settings where conventional infrastructure-intensive solutions may
not be possible. Its hardware light and scalable nature makes it an attractive option
for municipalities looking to implement smart city technologies at minimal cost of
implementation.
The system takes into account many essential aspects of quality pothole
monitoring in a seamless fusion of cutting-edge detection, localization, measurement,
storage, and visualization technologies. On its core, it relies on strong deep
learning-based object detection methods using well-annotated datasets of Roboflow
for accurate pothole discrimination against other analogous road defects. This greatly
reduces the risk of false positives and guarantees that the data gathered is accurate,
